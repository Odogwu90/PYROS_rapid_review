## 6. LinkedIn (personal post from Dr. Okpara; also usable on the page)

I trained as a doctor in Nigeria. I know the waiting room in this film.

For PYROS I led a rapid review of the research on a question I have lived: do skilled professionals who migrate and must start their careers again show more depressive symptoms than recent graduates who migrate before establishing a career?

Three things we found:

1. No published study has compared the two groups directly. The question affects millions of families and has not been tested.

2. Skilled migrants who are practising again look much like everyone else. Nurses in Germany and Finland showed no difference from local colleagues. Among Korean immigrants in Toronto, professionals had the lowest symptom scores of four occupational groups.

3. The risk sits in the gap between arriving and working at your level. Immigrants in the United States whose perceived social standing fell three or more rungs had three times the odds of a major depressive episode. A six-month placement for health-trained migrants in Norway improved general mental health and well-being, even before accreditation.

What this means for employers, licensing bodies and colleagues is in the film and the report. Full data and code are public.

Film (12 min): [YOUTUBE LINK]
Report, data and code: https://github.com/Odogwu90/PYROS_rapid_review

## 7. X / Twitter thread (post as one thread; 1/ is the hook)

1/ Ten years to become a doctor. Then a taxi.
We searched the research on what that does to the mind. Seven studies. One surprising gap. A thread, and a 12-minute film.

2/ Our question: does an experienced professional who migrates and starts over suffer more than a recent graduate who migrates with nothing to lose yet?

3/ First finding: nobody has ever compared them. Not one study. This question affects millions of families and has not been tested.

4/ Second: migrant nurses working in their field, in Germany and in Finland, looked no worse than local colleagues. In Toronto, immigrant professionals had the lowest symptom scores of four job groups.

5/ Third: the pain lives in the gap. Immigrants in the US whose social standing fell three or more rungs had three times the odds of a major depressive episode.

6/ Forced migrants working below their qualification had more depressive symptoms. "Working below your level hurts."

7/ Twenty Syrian doctors in Germany described the licence wait. One called the office more than five hundred times. Their words: depressed, irritated, in despair.

8/ What helped: a six-month placement for health-trained migrants in Norway, with a mentor from their profession, improved general mental health and well-being before any licence arrived. "I felt like myself again."

9/ Also: good colleagues and a real contract. Holding on to your professional identity. Learning the language. And how the host community treats you, the strongest predictor in one study.

10/ So who suffers more, the professional or the graduate? Honestly: nobody has measured it. The film ends on that question. 12 minutes, plain language, every number public.

**Reel 03 · Good news for migrant nurses**

**Reel 04 · Where does the pain live?**
Picture a ladder of society. Where did you stand at home? Where do you stand now? People who fell three or more rungs had three times the odds of a major depressive episode. The bigger the fall, the greater the risk. What softens the fall is in the full film. Link in bio.
#migration #mentalhealth #immigrantlife #skilledmigrants #PYROS

**Reel 05 · Working below your level hurts**
Two groups of university-educated forced migrants. In one, about one in four worked at their level. In the other, about seven in ten. Guess which group had more depressive symptoms. The full film explains why, and what helps. Link in bio.
#brainwaste #migration #mentalhealth #Ukraine #PYROS

**Reel 06 · He called 500 times. No answer.**
Syrian doctors in Germany described the licence wait: rules that changed monthly, calls never answered, unpaid placements refused. "It is the worst feeling ever." But the wall was the paperwork, not the profession. What finally helped is in the full film. Link in bio.
#doctorsabroad #migration #mentalhealth #Syria #PYROS

**Reel 07 · A piece of cloth decided her future**
Three women doctors were asked about their headscarf, not their training. Iranian students could not visit a sick parent without risking their whole degree. Years without seeing family. This is what the waiting feels like from inside. The full film shows the way out. Link in bio.
#immigrantlife #mentalhealth #migration #familyseparation #PYROS

**Reel 08 · What helps: get back in the room**
Doctors, pharmacists, a dentist and a nurse in Norway, all waiting for their licence, spent six months as assistant teachers with a mentor from their own profession. "I felt like myself again." Their children said: "My mother works at the university." Four more things that help are in the full film. Link in bio.
#skilledmigrants #mentalhealth #Norway #mentoring #PYROS

**Reel 09 · Good colleagues. A real contract. Your own name.**
Migrant nurses with good co-workers and permanent contracts did better. Refugee doctors who still saw themselves as doctors did better. Every hour of language learning is an investment in your mind. The fifth thing, and the strongest, is in the full film. Link in bio.
#migrantnurses #mentalhealth #migration #careersabroad #PYROS

**Reel 10 · Welcome is medicine**
Among Ukrainian women in Poland, the strongest predictor of depressive symptoms was not education or housing. It was how local people treated them. If you employ, license or live next to a skilled migrant: answer the phone, shorten the wait, make people feel wanted. The full film in bio.
#welcome #migration #mentalhealth #community #PYROS

**Reel 11 · How to read a study in 40 seconds**
Snapshots show two things go together; they cannot prove cause. Small studies are promising, not proven. Different questionnaires cannot be added up. We keep all three rules in the full film, so you can trust what it says. Link in bio.
#research #scienceliteracy #globalhealth #evidence #PYROS

**Reel 12 · So, who suffers more?**
The experienced professional or the recent graduate? Honestly: nobody has measured it. What the evidence does say is narrower and more useful: the danger is the time spent not working at your level, and it eases once you are. The whole story, 12 minutes, in bio.
